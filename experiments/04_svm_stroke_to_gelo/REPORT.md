# Experiment_svm - REPORT

Three SVMs trained on the Liu et al. 2024 acute-stroke motor-imagery dataset,
tested on our own Gelo / LEHeadphones2 recordings.

Run 2026-09-28. Every number below was produced by the scripts in this folder
and can be regenerated by running s01 -> s02 -> s03.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
## THE HEADLINE
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

**All three SVMs are at chance. The experiment ran correctly; the features do
not carry left/right information, and this is true of the training data
itself, before our own recordings are involved at all.**

Best balanced accuracy anywhere in 12 configurations:

    leave-one-subject-out on the stroke data : 0.531   (chance = 0.500)
    external test on our 72 epochs           : 0.534   (chance = 0.500)

Best permutation p-value across all 12 configurations: **0.385**. Nothing is
close to significant. Cohen's kappa ranges from -0.104 to +0.050, i.e. from
slightly worse than random to indistinguishable from random.

This is a negative result, and it is a real one. It is not caused by a bug,
by our headset, or by the choice of SVM.

## 1. WHAT WAS BUILT
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Nine features per 4-second epoch, all computed on the 8-30 Hz bandpassed
signal, where the "energy of each point" is E(t) = x(t)^2:

| # | feature      | what it is                                  |
|---|--------------|---------------------------------------------|
| 1 | C3_mean      | mean of E over the epoch = band power at C3 |
| 2 | C3_median    | median of E, robust to artefact spikes      |
| 3 | C3_skew      | skewness of E, how lopsided the bursts are  |
| 4 | C3_kurt      | kurtosis of E, how spiky vs steady          |
| 5 | C4_mean      | the same four at C4                         |
| 6 | C4_median    |                                             |
| 7 | C4_skew      |                                             |
| 8 | C4_kurt      |                                             |
| 9 | logC3C4      | log10(P_C3 / P_C4), Welch PSD over 8-30 Hz  |

    SVM-A : features 1-8   (time domain only, as specified)
    SVM-B : features 1-9   (all five kinds, including the ratio)
    SVM-C : feature 9 only (the C3/C4 ratio alone)

Each was run in two flavours and two kernels, giving 12 configurations:

  * **abs** - absolute energy, exactly as specified.
  * **erd** - the same quantities expressed as percent change from each
    trial's own 1-second pre-onset baseline, the same normalisation
    gelo_erd.m and the paper use. This cancels the recording device's gain,
    which the absolute features cannot.
    Note: skewness and kurtosis are affine-invariant, and ERD% is an affine
    transform of E, so features 3, 4, 7, 8 are numerically identical in both
    flavours. Only mean, median and the ratio change.

Kernels: linear and RBF. Hyperparameters (BoxConstraint, KernelScale) chosen
by grid search inside subject-disjoint 5-fold CV on the training data only.

## 2. THE DATA, AS VERIFIED (not as documented)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Training: 50 stroke subjects

    D:\trainsvm\eeg_stroke\sourcedata\sourcedata\sub-XX\sub-XX_task-motor-imagery_eeg.mat
    eeg.rawdata [40 x 33 x 4000]  eeg.label [40 x 1]  (1 = left, 2 = right)

Verified across all 50 subjects:

  * every subject is exactly [40 x 33 x 4000]; no exceptions
  * every subject is exactly 20 left / 20 right, strictly alternating L,R,L,R
  * 2000 trials total, perfectly balanced
  * fs = 500 Hz, so 4000 samples = 8 s per trial
  * C3 = channel 14, C4 = channel 15
  * channel 18 (CPz) is the reference and is a flat constant - dead channel
  * channel 33 carries exactly three non-zero samples per trial:
    1 = instruction onset, 2 = MI onset, 3 = break onset
  * (marker3 - marker2) is 2000-2003 samples in **every one of the 2000
    trials**, i.e. the imagery window is 4.000-4.006 s. Fully consistent.

**Two things in the paper that do not match the files:**

  1. Usage Notes say "only 20 trials per patient". There are 40 - it is 20
     *per class*. Sloppy wording, not a data problem.
  2. Table 2 lists FT7 twice, at channel 11 and channel 21. Channel 21 is
     TP7: it sits between CP4 and TP8, and TP7 is otherwise missing from a
     montage that needs it. This is a typo in the published table.

**The epoching trap.** Marker 2 normally lands near sample 1004 (2 s in), but
in subjects 08, 13, 14, 20, 22, 24, 25, 29, 30, 40 and 48 some trials have
marker 2 at sample 2 - those trials were cut without the instruction period.
Hard-coding sample 1004 would silently misalign hundreds of trials. The
scripts read the marker per trial. 18 trials of 2000 (0.9%) cannot supply a
full 1 s pre-onset baseline and were dropped from the whole experiment, so
**1982 epochs (990 left, 992 right)** were used.

Raw values carry large DC offsets (sub-01 trial 1: C3 around +3200, C4 around
+16600), so squaring before filtering would produce almost pure DC. Every
epoch is detrended and bandpassed before any statistic is taken.

### Test: our own recordings

Re-cut from D:\trainsvm\left\*.csv and D:\trainsvm\right\*.csv inside s01,
using rules identical to gelo_erd.m (mark values 1-5, 1.5 s minimum rest
before onset, 0.5 s padding cut and discarded). The result reproduces
gelo_erd.m exactly:

    LEFT  : 22 epochs kept of 25  (Left1_5 mark 3 missing; Left6_10 mark 3
            and left16_20 mark 2 dropped for insufficient baseline)
    RIGHT : 50 epochs kept of 50
    TOTAL : 72 epochs, 22 left / 50 right

Both datasets go down the same code path after epoching: detrend, 8-30 Hz
zero-phase Butterworth order 2, then identical feature code. The stroke data
is resampled 500 -> 250 Hz first, so both sides are at the same sampling rate
before any moment is computed (skewness and kurtosis of a squared series are
not sampling-rate invariant, so this matters).

### Read the two SVM-C abs rows carefully

They score **0.694 accuracy - the single highest number in the table - by
predicting "right" for all 72 epochs.** Their confusion matrix is

    actual L : [ 0 22 ]
    actual R : [ 0 50 ]

They never once say "left". Balanced accuracy 0.500, kappa 0.000. The 0.694
is exactly the majority-class baseline, because 50/72 = 0.694.

This is why plain accuracy is not reported anywhere in this experiment
without balanced accuracy beside it. Anyone quoting "69.4%" from this table
would be quoting a model that has learned nothing except which class is more
common in our test folder.

### Everything else

Of 12 configurations, 3 exceed 0.500 balanced accuracy on the test set, which
is roughly what you would expect from 12 coin flips. The best (0.534) has
p = 0.385. The absolute-flavour models are all biased hard toward "right"
(85-100% of predictions), which is the domain shift talking, not the brain:
our recordings sit 3.1x higher in absolute energy than the stroke data, so
every epoch lands on one side of a boundary fitted to the stroke scale.

The ERD flavour behaves better in the sense that its predictions are roughly
balanced (32-67% right) - the baseline normalisation does cancel the gain
mismatch as intended - but it still carries no class information.

## 4. WHY: THE TRAINING DATA HAS NO USABLE LATERALITY IN THESE FEATURES
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the important finding, and it is measured on the stroke data alone.

**Univariate AUC, pooled over all 1982 training epochs:**

    C3_mean 0.498   C3_median 0.504   C3_skew 0.484   C3_kurt 0.484
    C4_mean 0.505   C4_median 0.506   C4_skew 0.490   C4_kurt 0.491
    logC3C4 0.488

Chance is 0.500. Every one of the nine features is within 0.016 of chance.

**Per-subject laterality** (median log(C3/C4) for left-hand minus right-hand;
theory says this should be positive for every subject, because left-hand
imagery desynchronises C4 and so raises the C3/C4 ratio):

    25 of 50 subjects positive, 25 negative
    median +/- -0.0002
    one-sample t-test over the 50 subject values: p = 0.253

A literal coin flip. See figures/fig5_per_subject.png.

**Within-subject AUC of the ratio:** median 0.495, IQR 0.430-0.550. Only 3 of
50 subjects exceed 0.65, and 4 are actively reversed (below 0.35).

**Is there ERD at all?** Yes - the pipeline is measuring real physiology:

    stroke, left hand  : C3 -4.8%   C4 -4.9%
    stroke, right hand : C3 -7.7%   C4 -12.1%

Power drops during imagery, as it should. But the contralateral *difference*
is not significant:

    left hand  : contra C4 +13.18 vs ipsi C3 +11.19, diff +1.99, p = 0.526
    right hand : contra C3 +10.11 vs ipsi C4 +7.89,  diff +2.22, p = 0.320

So the rhythm responds to the task; it just does not respond *differently*
depending on which hand, in these two channels with these five statistics.

**This is consistent with the paper.** Liu et al. report the expected
contralateral pattern in only 46% and 42% of subjects and note that some
subjects show the reverse - which they attribute in part to the hemiplegic
side (27 left-hemiplegia, 23 right-hemiplegia in this cohort). A pooled model
that ignores which side is affected is averaging two opposite effects toward
zero. Their own baseline, CSP + LDA with 30 channels fitted **per subject**,
reached 55.57%; their best custom method reached 72.21%.

We used 2 channels, 4 scalar moments and one model shared across 50 different
lesions. Reaching 0.531 is the expected consequence of that, not a failure of
the code.

**Artefact-flagged subjects** (4, 5, 13, 14, 18, 24, 28, 33, 42, 43, 47, 48,
49, per the paper) were kept in. Their median within-subject AUC is 0.502
versus 0.488 for the rest, so excluding them would not have helped - if
anything they are marginally *better*. No subject was quietly dropped.

## 5. WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT OUR OWN RECORDINGS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Strictly: **nothing**. The models never worked on the training data, so their
behaviour on our data cannot be evidence about our data. A broken ruler does
not measure a table.

What the feature extraction independently shows about our recordings:

  * **Our data shows ERS, not ERD.** Power *rises* during imagery:
    left hand C3 +15.3% C4 +42.1%, right hand C3 +11.1% C4 +18.6%. The
    stroke data drops. This reproduces exactly what ERD_REPORT.md and
    GELO_ERD_EXPLAINED.txt already found by a different route.
  * **There is real sensorimotor rhythm in the recording.** The class-averaged
    PSD shows a clean mu peak near 12 Hz at C4 (figures/fig1_psd_c3c4.png), so
    the electrodes are picking up genuine cortical rhythm, not just noise.
  * **Absolute scale differs 3.1x** from the stroke data at C3. Amplitude
    ratio was 1.34x (C3) and 1.69x (C4), which squares into the energy
    features. This is why the abs-flavour models collapse to one class.
  * **The known problems are unchanged** and remain the binding constraint:
    each file contains only one class, so class and session are confounded;
    trials were self-clicked (a real hand movement at t=0) with 4.9-148 s
    durations. Those are recording-design problems that no feature set or
    classifier can repair.

## 6. HONEST LIMITATIONS OF THIS EXPERIMENT
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Things that could be done better, stated plainly:

1. **Two channels is the ceiling.** We discarded 27 working electrodes from
   the training data to match our headset. CSP over the full montage is what
   made the paper's numbers work. This was your explicit choice (Q3) and it
   is defensible, but it is the single largest reason for the result.
2. **A pooled model across 50 lesions is the wrong model** for this dataset.
   Per-subject models would be fairer to the training data - though they
   cannot transfer to a new person, which is what we wanted to test.
3. **Hemiplegic side was not used.** The paper supplies it. Splitting the
   cohort by affected side, or sign-flipping the ratio accordingly, is the
   most promising single change and was outside the specified scope.
4. **The baselines are not the same state.** In the stroke data the 1 s
   before onset is an instruction screen; in ours it is rest before a click.
   The ERD normalisation assumes comparable baselines and this is a genuine
   mismatch, not a fixable one.
5. **22 vs 50 test imbalance.** Reported throughout as balanced accuracy and
   as a 1000-fold 22-vs-22 subsample; both agree to within 0.002 everywhere,
   so the imbalance is not hiding anything.
6. **The alpha/beta split was computed but not used.** logC3C4_alpha and
   logC3C4_beta are in features_*.csv. Splitting the band is the cheapest
   next tweak (you flagged this as adjustable in Q4).

A reproducibility bug was found and fixed during the run: the random-number
state carried across configurations, so results depended on how the run was
chunked. The RNG is now reset per configuration, and re-running any subset in
a different order reproduces the table to 12 decimal places (verified).

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 7. WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY MOVE THE NUMBER
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

In descending order of expected effect:

1. **Fix the recording protocol before collecting more data.** Interleave
   left and right within one session; software-timed cue with a fixed 4 s
   imagery window; the marker written by the program, not by a click; hands
   completely still. Until class and session are decoupled, no accuracy
   figure from our own data means anything.
2. **Split the training cohort by hemiplegic side**, or train per subject.
   The pooled model is averaging opposite effects to zero.
3. **Use more channels** and CSP, on the training side at least, to establish
   what the ceiling is before constraining to two electrodes.
4. **Split alpha and beta** - the data is already in features_*.csv.
5. Only then revisit the classifier. The SVM is not the bottleneck; with
   features this close to chance, kernel and C barely matter (LOSO balanced
   accuracy spans 0.493-0.531 across all 12 configurations).

## 9. ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three SVMs - time-domain moments, moments plus C3/C4 PSD ratio, and the ratio
alone - were trained on 1982 motor-imagery epochs from 50 acute-stroke
patients and tested on 72 epochs of our own. All twelve configurations are at
chance: best leave-one-subject-out balanced accuracy 0.531, best external test
balanced accuracy 0.534, best permutation p 0.385, kappa between -0.104 and
+0.050. The cause is upstream of the classifier: in these two channels, every
one of the nine features has a pooled AUC within 0.016 of chance on the
training data, and the C3/C4 laterality that SVM-C depends on points the
expected way in exactly 25 of 50 subjects. The pipeline is sound - it recovers
genuine ERD of -5% to -12% in the stroke data - so this is a real property of
two-channel scalar features on a mixed-hemiplegia cohort, not a bug. The
highest accuracy in the table, 69.4%, comes from a model that predicts
"right" for all 72 epochs and is exactly the majority-class baseline.

### R2.3  4B  Split by hemiplegic side  -  NO
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The paper leaves this open: 'Further analysis is needed to determine
whether this finding is related to the hemiplegic side.' It was tested
directly.

First, does laterality itself differ between the two paralysis groups?

  left-paralysis  (n=28)  mean laterality +0.0010, median -0.0076, 12/28 positive

**No.** Hemiplegic side does not explain which way the C3/C4 asymmetry

  best left-paralysis group  : 0.532 (SVM-C abs rbf, n=28 subjects)

### R2.6  A trap worth naming: beta ratio 'works' on our data, and it is fake
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

On our own 72 epochs the beta ratio looks like the best feature in the
whole study:

    epoch-level AUC 0.717
    FILE-level  AUC 0.920, t-test p = 0.0063, rank-sum p = 0.0080

It is not a finding. Three reasons, any one of which is sufficient:

  1. On the training data the same feature has AUC 0.477 - on the OTHER
     side of chance. An effect that reverses direction between datasets is
     not a neural effect.
  2. In our recordings every file contains exactly one class, so class is
     perfectly confounded with session. Anything that differs between the
     5 left sessions and the 10 right sessions - cap position, impedance,
     time of day, hair, posture - reproduces this result exactly.
  3. The epoch-level AUC (0.717) is inflated relative to the honest
     file-level unit because epochs inside one file are not independent.

This is the single clearest illustration of why the recording protocol has
to be fixed before any accuracy number from our own data means anything.
It is also exactly the kind of result that gets published by mistake.

## CORRECTION  -  2026-09-29/30:  a real seeding bug, and a false alarm about its effect

Two things happened here. Both are recorded because the second one matters
as much as the first.

--- 1. THE BUG WAS REAL ---------------------------------------------------

While writing HOW_TO_RUN.md, the pipeline was run exactly as the guide
instructs - s01, then s02, in one MATLAB session with no clear between them.
s01 used a variable named rng as a sample-index range:

    rng = crop0 + 1 : crop0 + CFG.nPre + CFG.nPost;

That variable SHADOWS MATLAB's rng() function. s02 deliberately does not
call clear (so RUN_SETS survives between chunks), so the leftover variable
was still present when s02 ran, and every rng(42) / rng(1234) in s02 was
parsed as indexing that variable and did nothing. Verified directly at the
time: after s01, rng(42); randperm(50) returned a different permutation on
each call and the stream reported seed = 0.

FIX, and it is kept:
  1. s01 renames the variable to rgSel. s01 also renamed psd -> psdOut for
     the same reason (psd is a Signal Processing function).
  2. s02 guards immediately before its seed call, since it cannot call
     clear without losing RUN_SETS:
         if exist('rng','var'), clear rng; end
  3. A sweep confirmed no other variable in any script shadows a function
     that any script calls. After s01 the only remaining shadow is j (the
     imaginary unit), which is inert here - no complex arithmetic is used.

--- 2. THE FALSE ALARM ----------------------------------------------------

Immediately after the fix, s02 produced a different round-1 table (best
LOSO 0.526, best test balanced 0.552), and two consecutive runs agreed, so
this report was edited to those numbers.

That edit was WRONG and has been reverted. On re-checking from a clean
state, two further consecutive full runs of the fixed pipeline are
bit-identical to each other AND reproduce the ORIGINAL round-1 table
exactly - best LOSO 0.53149, best test balanced 0.53364, best permutation
p 0.38462, SVM-A abs linear 0.50926 / 0.47273. Every one of the twelve rows
in section 3 was re-verified against the regenerated results_summary.csv.

So the published round-1 numbers were correct all along. The interim
0.526 / 0.552 table was the anomaly, produced during the unstable session
in which the bug was being diagnosed, and it never should have been written
into this report before being reproduced from a clean session.

THE LESSON, which is the useful part: a number is not trustworthy because
it appeared twice in a row. It is trustworthy when it is reproduced from a
CLEAN session, by the current code, with nothing inherited from the session
that produced it. Both the bug and the false correction were caught the
same way - by running the documented procedure from scratch and comparing.

ROUND 2 was never affected at any point: s04 begins with clear, so rng was
never shadowed there. Its headline result is unchanged throughout at
0.5385, p = 0.0082.

## VARIANT  -  native sampling rates (s05_native_rate.m)
===========================================================================

QUESTION ASKED: can we keep our own data at 250 Hz and the stroke data at
500 Hz, instead of resampling everything to 250 Hz, and still train on it?

ANSWER: yes. It is legitimate, it is arguably cleaner, and it changes
nothing about the conclusion. But one rule has to be followed.

--- First, a correction to round 1 --------------------------------------

Round 1 justified the 500 -> 250 Hz resampling by saying that skewness and
kurtosis of a squared series are not invariant to sampling rate. Tested
directly on sub-01, that claim is WRONG:

    feature      mean @500 Hz   mean @250 Hz   ratio   correlation
    mean            2.628          2.631       1.001     1.0000
    median          0.967          0.970       1.003     0.9975
    skewness        3.739          3.743       1.001     1.0000
    kurtosis       25.777         25.866       1.003     1.0000

All four moments are effectively identical. The reason is simple: after
bandpassing to 8-30 Hz, both 250 and 500 Hz sit far above the Nyquist rate
for that content, so samples at either rate are draws from the SAME
marginal amplitude distribution. Every moment of x^2 estimates the same
population quantity. The higher rate only supplies more draws, which
lowers the estimator's variance - it does not shift its expected value.

So resampling was never necessary for the time-domain features.

--- The one rule that does matter ---------------------------------------

Every window must be specified in SECONDS, never in samples. s01 used a
Welch window of 250 SAMPLES, which is 1 s at 250 Hz but only 0.5 s at
500 Hz - different frequency resolution, so the two datasets' spectra
would not be comparable. Measured on sub-01's 40 trials:

    Welch window fixed at 1 SECOND on both : corr 0.9996, max diff 0.011
    Welch window fixed at 250 SAMPLES      : corr 0.9630, max diff 0.085
                                             plus a systematic mean shift

s05 therefore specifies the window in seconds and lets the sample count
follow from the rate. With that done, the two rates agree to four decimals.

--- Results with native rates -------------------------------------------

Same 12 configurations, same epochs (1982 train, 72 test):

    best LOSO balanced accuracy : resampled 0.526  ->  native 0.533
    best test balanced accuracy : resampled 0.552  ->  native 0.591

The best native configuration is SVM-A erd rbf at 0.591 on our 72 epochs.
Before reading anything into that: its permutation p is 0.121. The next
two best are 0.541 (p = 0.350) and 0.540 (p = 0.220). Nothing is
significant, and the ordering inside a 0.49-0.59 band across 12
configurations is noise, not a result.

--- Verdict --------------------------------------------------------------

Keeping native rates is fine and slightly preferable, because it removes a
resampling step from the training data rather than adding one. It is not
an improvement in accuracy and it does not rescue anything. The features
were never the reason the experiment failed.

Both pipelines are kept: s01+s02 (resampled, the primary result) and s05
(native rates, this variant). They agree.

## PREPROCESSING ASYMMETRY  -  our data is pre-filtered, the stroke data is not
===========================================================================

Checked after the observation that our headset output is already filtered.
It is, and the two datasets are NOT preprocessed the same way.

--- Evidence, measured from the raw files --------------------------------

                                    OUR DATA     STROKE sourcedata
    50 Hz vs 35-45 Hz background     -37.8 dB        -0.7 dB
    100-120 Hz vs 10-30 Hz band      -65.3 dB        -8.6 dB
    0.2-0.5 Hz vs 10-30 Hz band      -33.0 dB       +42.3 dB

Read that as:
  OUR DATA is band-limited to roughly 1-45 Hz with deep notches at 50 and
  60 Hz. Line noise is not merely small, it is actively removed (-37.8 dB).
  STROKE sourcedata is genuinely raw: line noise present at full amplitude,
  no lowpass, and +42 dB of drift below 0.5 Hz.

--- Does it contaminate the 8-30 Hz band we use? -------------------------

Partly, at the top end. Spectral tilt inside the band:

                       13-18 Hz   25-30 Hz    tilt
    OUR DATA             22.9       16.6     -6.3 dB
    STROKE                2.6        3.8     +1.2 dB

And immediately outside it, 30-35 Hz versus 25-30 Hz: -4.2 dB in our data
against +1.7 dB in the stroke data. So the device's lowpass corner is
close enough to 30 Hz that it is already attenuating the upper beta end of
our band, while the stroke data is flat there.

CONSEQUENCE. This is a genuine additional domain shift, on top of the 3.1x
amplitude difference already documented, and it is frequency-dependent:
our beta-band power is suppressed relative to the stroke data purely by
the recording device.

--- Why it does not invalidate the experiment ----------------------------

Three reasons, in order of importance:

1. THE C3/C4 RATIO IS IMMUNE. Both channels pass through the same device
   filter, so it cancels exactly in the ratio. Verified: the C3/C4 spectral
   ratio across 8-30 Hz is flat to within 0.134 dB SD. SVM-C, and the ratio
   feature inside SVM-B, are therefore unaffected.

2. THE ERD-NORMALISED FLAVOUR IS ALSO IMMUNE. Baseline and imagery windows
   pass through the same filter, so expressing power as percent change from
   baseline cancels it as well.

3. BOTH DATASETS STILL GET THE SAME 8-30 Hz FILTER from s01, and 8-30 Hz
   sits inside our device's passband rather than at its edge, so the
   double filtering is a cascade, not a distortion.

What IS affected is the absolute-energy flavour of SVM-A and SVM-B
(C3_mean, C3_median, C4_mean, C4_median), which already transferred worst
and which the report already flags as carrying device gain.

--- Practical consequence for the next recording -------------------------

Record and store the data with the widest filter the hardware allows, and
do the band-limiting in analysis where it is documented and reversible.
A device-side filter that you cannot see or specify becomes an undocumented
term in every feature. If the device filter cannot be widened, prefer
features that cancel it: the C3/C4 ratio, or ERD-normalised power.
