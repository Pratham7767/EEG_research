1. The big picture

The app is a chain of small steps. The headset measures voltages; the vendor's software (the Capsule SDK, a closed-source Windows DLL) cleans them and computes some scores; our Python code computes the rest, decides when to prompt you, draws the screen and saves everything.

There are two separate analysis paths in our code, because the SDK's spectrum cannot show the slowest and fastest waves (section 7):

2. The headset and its signal

How

Four dry electrodes: A1 and A2 behind the ears, C3 and C4 on top of the head. The SDK is set to bipolar mode, so it reports two channels: A1-C3 (left) and A2-C4 (right). Each channel is sampled 250 times per second.

Why

A bipolar channel subtracts two electrodes, which cancels noise they share (for example from the body or the room). The SDK's classifiers expect bipolar mode.

From where

Vendor Channel names, sample rate and mode come from the SDK at connection time (Device.get_channel_names, get_eeg_sample_rate). Electrode names follow the international 10-20 system Literature. Your device: "Headphone", serial 830817.

3. Connecting

How

The app asks the SDK's DeviceLocator to search for 10 s, lists what it finds, and connects to the one you pick. If connecting fails it retries after 2, 5 and 10 s. Everything the SDK reports (new data, status changes) arrives through callbacks, which only run when the app calls the SDK's update(); the app does that every 20 ms from the window's own timer.

Why

The SDK is single-threaded: calling it from one place avoids crashes and lets the window stay responsive. Bluetooth connections sometimes fail on the first try, so automatic retries save the user a restart.

From where

Vendor Connection flow from the SDK documentation (Capsule flow, Device connection pages) and the vendor's Python examples. Design choice The 10 s scan and the 2/5/10 s retry delays.

4. Electrode contact (the Signal step)

How

The SDK measures each electrode's resistance (how well it touches the skin). The app grades it: Good below 500 kΩ, Fair 500–1000 kΩ, Poor above. You can continue once every electrode has been Good or Fair for 5 seconds in a row. During the session, the SDK also flags each moment as artifact or clean; if more than 30% of the last 30 s is flagged, the screen says "Adjust headphones" and those seconds are left out of your averages.

Why

Poor contact lets in noise that looks like brain activity. The 5-second hold stops a single lucky reading from opening the gate.

From where

Vendor The 500 kΩ / 1 MΩ thresholds are quoted from the SDK documentation page Devices modes (Docs/html/devices_modes.html): "Above 1000 kOhm - bad quality, 500 to 1000 kOhm - average quality, Below 500 kOhm - good quality". Artifact flags: SDK EEGArtifacts. Design choice The 5 s hold, the 30 s window and the 30% warning level.

5. Calibration: your alpha peak

How

You close your eyes for 30 seconds. The SDK's CalibrateIndividualNFBQuick finds the frequency where your alpha rhythm is strongest: the individual alpha peak frequency (iAPF). It returns the peak and an individual alpha range. A result code tells whether it worked (0 = OK, 1 = too many artifacts, 2 = peak on the edge of the range).

Why

Alpha is strongest with eyes closed, so that is the easiest moment to find its peak. People's alpha peaks differ (roughly 8–13 Hz) and the SDK tunes its later calculations to yours.

From where

Vendor Method and the 30-second closed-eyes procedure: SDK documentation page Calibration ("CalibrateIndividualNFBQuick will require 1 stage: Closed eyes - 30 sec") and the vendor's Instruction Manual.docx. Literature The vendor cites Bazanova (2012) and Posthuma et al. (2001) for iAPF. Your result: 11.7 Hz (13:52 session).

6. Rest baseline (eyes open)

How

You look at a cross for 30–90 seconds. The SDK's Productivity module records your resting levels (StartBaselineCalibration) and moves on by itself. At the same time, our code stores the median power of each brain-wave band from the raw EEG; that is the reference for the brain-wave bars.

Why

All scores are relative. "Attention 70" only means something compared with how you were at rest.

From where

Vendor SDK Productivity metrics page and Instruction Manual ("Immediately after the 30-second window, click Calibrate Baseline to set the reference point"). Design choice Using the same period as the band reference.

7. The SDK spectrum (PSD)

A power spectrum (PSD, power spectral density) splits the signal into frequencies and says how strong each is. The SDK sends one for each channel about 23 times per second, in 0.1 Hz bins from 1.0 to 30.0 Hz: 291 numbers per channel. One bin is one 0.1 Hz slice.

How

The app reads 2 spectra per second. For each band it adds the bins with lo ≤ f < hi (θ 4.0–6.9: 30 bins; α 7.0–12.9: 60; β 15.0–29.9: 150; high β 20.0–29.9: 100; total 1.0–29.9: 290), divides each band by the channel's total (relative power), and averages the two channels.

Why

Dividing by the total removes changes in overall loudness (contact getting better or worse), leaving the balance between rhythms. The "< hi" rule stops a border bin from counting twice.

From where

Vendor Band ranges from the vendor's Metrics API 2024.xlsx (θ 4–7, α 7–12/13, β 15–30 Hz; SMR 13–15 Hz is a separate vendor band) and the SDK's own band edges. We kept them so our ratios match the vendor's formulas. Measured Bin spacing and count read from the SDK. Measured The SDK filters its signal: measured on the simulator (white noise in, compare processed with raw), the chart below. The SDK documentation confirms an "effective EEG measurement bandwidth set from 4 to 35 Hz".

8. Attention, Cognitive load, Relaxation (vendor)

How

These three come straight from the SDK's Emotions classifier as 0–100 values. Our code only clips them to 0–100 and shows the median of the last 5 seconds.

Why

The vendor designed and tuned these for this headset. Keeping them lets you compare with the vendor's own apps.

From where

Vendor SDK Emotions page (fields attention, relaxation, cognitiveLoad, cognitiveControl, selfControl). The vendor's Capsule metrics_definitions.xlsx describes them as normalised β/α (attention), normalised α/β (relaxation) and normalised θ/α (cognitive load), citing Raufi & Longo (2022) for θ/α Literature. The exact normalisation is not published. Measured Checked on your first session: the vendor's raw concentration and relaxation match our β/α and α/β (Spearman ρ = 0.84 and 0.85), but the 0–100 versions only moderately (ρ ≈ 0.5–0.6).

Limit. In the 18:09 session the vendor's cognitive load rose from 42 to 70 while θ/α of the same spectrum fell from 1.10 to 0.79. We cannot see why inside the DLL.

9. Stress

Step 1: the ratio

arousal = relative high β (20–30 Hz) / relative α (7–13 Hz)
How

Computed from each SDK spectrum, 2 times per second.

Why

Fast beta rises and alpha falls when you are tense or aroused, so their ratio goes up.

From where

Literature High-β relative to α is a common arousal/anxiety marker. But a meta-analysis of stress EEG studies (Vanhollebeke et al., 2022) found no consistent beta effect and mixed alpha effects, and EEG above ~20 Hz contains muscle activity (Whitham et al., 2007). Design choice Using this ratio as the stress score. Treat it as "arousal compared with your own normal", not as a clinical stress measure.

Step 2: the reference, your first 2 minutes of work

How

For the first 120 s of the session (breaks and poor-signal moments do not count) the app stores every ratio and takes the median. The Stress card shows "Starts in m:ss" meanwhile.

Why

Measured In your 13:52 session the ratio was about 0.58 while looking at the cross and about 1.1 during screen work. Compared with rest, ordinary work already looked like stress (the first version showed a stress episode for 3.5 of 4.4 minutes). Looking at a busy screen lowers alpha. The median ignores single spikes.

From where

Measured The mismatch was found in your recording. Literature Time-on-task studies also compare with the start of the task rather than with rest. Design choice The 2-minute length.

Step 3: ratio to score

score = 50 + 50 × tanh( log₂( ratio / reference ) )
How

log₂ turns "double" into +1 and "half" into −1, so rises and falls are symmetric. tanh squeezes any value into −1…+1, so the score stays within 0–100 and one wild reading cannot jump to the end.

Why

A fixed, simple mapping everyone can check by hand, with 50 always meaning "same as your reference".

From where

Design choice Our own mapping. Check points: ½× → 12, 1× → 50, 1.6× → 80, 2× → 88.

Worked example (13:52 session, 3 minutes in). Relative powers: θ 0.195, α 0.336, β 0.412, high β 0.286.

arousal = 0.286 / 0.336 = 0.850 ratio/ref = 0.850 / 0.798 = 1.066 log₂ = 0.093 tanh = 0.092 score = 50 + 50 × 0.092 = 54.6

Step 4: smoothing and stress episodes

How

The card shows the median of the last 30 s of scores. A stress episode starts when that stays ≥ 80 for 30 s and ends when it stays < 65 for 30 s. Episodes end when a break starts.

Why

Measured On your recording, even 10-second medians of the ratio swing by about ±47% (standard deviation 0.56 in log₂). The first version's thresholds (70 for 15 s) sat inside that normal wobble. Different start/end levels (hysteresis) stop an episode flickering on and off.

From where

Measured Chosen by replaying your 13:52 recording under several settings: with these, ordinary work gives no episode. Design choice Not yet validated against a real stress task (plan.md §6.5).

10. Fatigue

fatigue ratio = (relative θ + relative α) / relative β
How

Same steps as stress: SDK spectrum → ratio → reference = median of the first 2 minutes of work → the same 0–100 mapping → median of the last 60 s.

Why

Slow waves (θ, α) grow relative to β as you tire during a long task. Tiredness builds over minutes, so a longer smoothing window suits it.

From where

Vendor The formula (α+θ)/β is the vendor's Fatigue Score (Capsule metrics_definitions.xlsx, citing Tyas et al., 2020, and Jap et al., 2009 Literature). Literature Theta and alpha rise with time on task (Psychophysiology, 2021). Design choice 60 s smoothing and the work reference.

Worked example (same moment):

ratio = (0.195 + 0.336) / 0.412 = 1.288 vs ref = 1.288 / 1.076 = 1.197 → score = 62.7

Why not the vendor's fatigue? The SDK also offers "accumulated fatigue". In your first session it grew at its fastest rate whenever concentration was above 1.7, and your resting concentration was already 2.2, so it reached 6/10 in 3.3 minutes and asked for a break. It is still saved in productivity.csv but not used.

11. Brain-wave bars (our own spectrum)

The SDK spectrum has no delta or gamma left (section 7), so this path starts from the raw EEG, which the SDK passes through unfiltered (1–120 Hz according to its documentation).

Step 1: filter

How

A 2nd-order Butterworth high-pass at 0.5 Hz plus a notch at 50 Hz (Q = 30), combined into one filter and run continuously, block by block, keeping the filter's memory between blocks.

Why

The high-pass removes very slow drift (sweat, electrodes settling) while keeping delta (1–4 Hz). The notch removes hum from the mains wiring, which in India is 50 Hz.

From where

Literature Standard IIR designs from SciPy (butter, iirnotch, sosfilt). Measured Earlier raw recordings from these headphones had 50 Hz hum up to 290× stronger than the 40–45 Hz level. Measured Tested: hum reduced by more than 30 dB and does not leak into gamma.

Step 2: windows and rejection

How

Every 0.5 s, take the newest 4 s (1000 samples). Skip the window if the SDK flagged an artifact inside it, or if its peak-to-peak amplitude is more than 4× the median of earlier windows (after the first 8).

Why

Blinks and movements add huge slow waves that would swamp the real ones. A relative threshold is used because dry electrodes give hundreds of µV even when clean, so a fixed µV limit would reject everything.

From where

Vendor SDK artifact flags. Design choice 4 s, 0.5 s step, 4× and 8 windows.

Step 3: Welch spectrum

How

Three 2-second segments with 50% overlap, each tapered with a Hann window and linearly detrended, then averaged. Bin spacing = 1 / 2 s = 0.5 Hz.

Why

A single spectrum of noisy data jumps around; averaging three is steadier. 2 s segments are the trade-off between detail (0.5 Hz bins, enough to separate the bands) and steadiness.

From where

Literature Welch (1967), implemented by SciPy signal.welch. Design choice Segment and window lengths.

Step 4: band power

band power (µV²) = Σ PSD(f) × Δf over lo ≤ f < hi, Δf = 0.5 Hz δ 1–4 (6 bins) · θ 4–8 (8) · α 8–13 (10) · β 13–30 (34) · γ 30–45 (30)
How

The PSD is a density (µV² per Hz); multiplying by the bin width gives power. The two channels are averaged.

Why

These bands are the textbook ones, so the chart reads like any EEG textbook. γ stops at 45 Hz, below the 50 Hz hum.

From where

Literature Band definitions from the IFCN glossary (Kane et al., 2017). They differ slightly from the vendor ranges in section 7, which are only used for the stress path.

Step 5: compare with rest

bar = ( median power in the last 5 s / median power during the rest baseline − 1 ) × 100 %
How

Each band is compared with its own power at rest. +50% = one and a half times, −50% = half.

Why

Measured The first version compared each band's share of total power. During your rest baseline delta held 75% of the power (freshly placed electrodes). As the electrodes settled, delta shrank and every other band's share rose automatically, so alpha, beta and gamma all showed about +75%.

From where

Literature Percent change of band power from a reference period is the standard event-related (de)synchronisation measure (Pfurtscheller & Lopes da Silva, 1999). Measured 5 s smoothing: on your data the bars then move about 2 points per second.

12. Breaks and check-ins

How

A break prompt appears when (A) 50 minutes pass without a real break, or (B) the fatigue score stays ≥ 70 for 3 minutes in a row after the first 10 minutes. At most one prompt per 10 minutes, so an ignored prompt repeats. A break must last 2 minutes to restart the 50-minute timer. Nothing is prompted during a break or for 10 minutes after it, and fatigue measured during a break does not count. Every 10 minutes a sleepiness check-in (1–9) appears, except during a break.

Why

The timer is a safety net that works even when the EEG cannot see tiredness; the fatigue rule adds a personal signal. The check-in answers are the ground truth for testing whether the fatigue score works.

From where

Literature Karolinska Sleepiness Scale (Åkerstedt & Gillberg, 1990). Research on rest breaks and EEG fatigue (Int. J. Psychophysiology 2023; Rest Assured, CHI 2026) motivated regular breaks. Measured The 2-minute minimum: in your session a 1.5-second break had restarted the timer. Design choice 50 min, 70 for 3 min, 10 min cooldown: common-sense defaults, not yet validated.

13. Recording

How

Each session gets a folder named by date and time with CSV files (one row per reading) and a session.json summary: scores.csv at 1 per second, bands.csv at 2 per second, events.csv for markers, breaks, prompts and episodes, plus the SDK's raw outputs.

Why

Everything on screen can be recomputed and checked later. This explainer's figures are rebuilt from these files.

From where

Design choice File layout. Python's csv and json modules.

14. Testing

How

80 automated tests (pytest). Unit tests feed made-up signals with known answers; simulator tests run the real SDK with its simulated devices; a self-test drives the real window through all seven steps.

Why

A known input must give a known output before real brain data is trusted.

From where

Measured Examples: white noise must give each band a share equal to its width (within 3%); a pure 10 Hz tone must be ≥ 95% alpha; the notch must cut 50 Hz by more than 30 dB; slow drift at rest must not move the other bands. Vendor The SDK's Noise and SinWave simulated devices.

15. Known issues

A noisy rest baseline makes every bar negative

In the 18:09 session the SDK flagged 49 of 90 windows during the eyes-open baseline as artifacts, and the slow waves were several times stronger than a minute later (theta 272 µV² at rest against 60 µV² in the session). The rest reference was therefore far too high, so during the session every band read lower (delta −92%, theta −83%, alpha −69%). The amplitude check did not catch it: it learns "normal" from the first windows, and the signal was already large then.

Proposed fix (not yet built): check the baseline's quality before accepting it, and repeat it if more than about 20% is artifact.

Vendor scores are a black box

Attention, cognitive load and relaxation cannot be checked step by step (section 8). A possible change is to replace them with our own β/α and θ/α computed like stress.

Stress and fatigue are not yet validated

The thresholds were tuned on short test sessions. The planned check (plan.md §6.5) is mental arithmetic vs rest for stress, and the sleepiness check-ins vs the fatigue score over long sessions.

17. Check yourself

Try to answer from memory before looking at the answers below. If one is hard, reread the section in brackets.

  1. Why can't the brain-wave bars use the SDK's spectrum? (7, 11)
  2. The SDK spectrum has 0.1 Hz bins. How many bins does the alpha band (7–13 Hz) add up, and why is the 13.0 Hz bin not one of them? (7)
  3. A ratio is 1.5× your reference. Without a calculator, is the score above or below 80? (9)
  4. Why is stress compared with your first 2 minutes of work and not with the eyes-open rest? (9)
  5. Our Welch segments are 2 s long. What bin spacing does that give, and what would 4 s segments give? (11)
  6. Delta drops by half while the other bands stay the same. What would the old share-based chart have shown for alpha, roughly? What does the current chart show? (11)
  7. You take a 90-second break at minute 45. When does the 50-minute prompt come? (12)
  8. Which three numbers on screen can you not recompute by hand from the saved data, and why? (8)

Answers

  1. The SDK filters its signal to about 4–30 Hz before computing the spectrum, so almost no delta (gain 0.004 at 1–2 Hz) or gamma is left.
  2. 60 bins (7.0, 7.1 … 12.9). The rule is lo ≤ f < hi, so 13.0 is outside; that keeps a border bin from counting in two bands.
  3. Below. 1.6× gives 80, so 1.5× gives a little less: log₂ 1.5 = 0.585, tanh = 0.526, score ≈ 76.
  4. Looking at a busy screen lowers alpha, so high β / α is higher during any screen work than at rest. Compared with rest, ordinary work already looked like stress (first session: an "episode" for 3.5 of 4.4 minutes).
  5. 1 / 2 s = 0.5 Hz. 4 s segments would give 0.25 Hz bins, but only one segment would fit in the 4 s window, so the spectrum would be much noisier.
  6. If delta was most of the power (say 75%), halving it makes the total smaller, so every other band's share rises by roughly half (about +60%). The current chart compares each band with its own rest power: alpha 0%.
  7. At minute 50. 90 s is shorter than the 2-minute minimum, so the timer was not restarted. (A 2-minute break would have moved it to about minute 97.)
  8. Attention, Cognitive load and Relaxation. They come from the vendor's closed Emotions classifier, whose normalisation is not published.
